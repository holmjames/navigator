Navigatoran agent team for 500 reps

Navigator

A team of agents that does the market, account, and prospect research, drafts and checks every touch, handles replies, and tells each rep what to do next.

Designed for 500 BDRs, built to run at 1,000. A concept for a 500-rep sales org, prepared by James Holm, September 2026.

  • Quote it as is or not at all

    Agents personalize only with receipts. For a public company that is a verbatim quote from a call or filing. For a private one it is the artifact itself: a job posting, a press release, a funding announcement. No artifact, no personalization; the touch runs on fit and timing.

  • Research once, use many

    Account briefs live in Snowflake with a freshness date. Nothing gets re-researched inside 14 days unless a new signal fires.

  • Autonomy by tier

    SMB touches send on their own within caps. Mid-market drafts wait for a rep. Enterprise gets research and warm paths, and a human writes.

  • Outcomes retrain the score

    Every reply, meeting, and opp flows back. Weights get recalibrated monthly against what converted, and QA grades get checked against reply rates.

  • Enable reps, don't replace them

    Every rep starts the day with a next best action for each account: who to contact, on which channel, and the receipt that makes it worth doing now. Agents handle the research and first drafts. Reps keep the judgment, the relationships, and the conversations.

What happens to a record at each stage, and how often.

Clay pushes enriched rows and fires a webhook when a play matches. Salesforce syncs nightly. Apollo and Nooks post events as they happen. TheSwarm paths refresh weekly per account.
Resolveon arrivalEach account row is matched to one account_id through the identifier ladder and to its Salesforce id. Contacts key on account_id plus LinkedIn URL. Ambiguous or conflicting rows go to a review queue; nothing is guessed, and a subsidiary carrying its parent's website is caught here.
Storehourly buildsRaw tables are append-only and keep the source URL on every signal. dbt builds the core tables and the marts the playbook and the manager view read. A signal without a source URL never gets past raw.
Scorenightly, and on every signalSQL computes fit, signal, timing, and relationship. Tier follows from segment; priority from the score. The model turns the components into three plain-language reasons a rep can read in five seconds.
Decideevent-drivenThe orchestrator wakes for an account when its score or signals change. It checks suppression, send caps, and whether the brief is still fresh. Only then do agents run, and only the ones needed.

Messaging drafts from receipted facts only. QA grades; below B goes back for a rewrite. Agents read the cache first and call vendors only for what is missing.
Act7 AM local, then liveA card lands in Slack. Tier A sends within caps. Tier B waits for a tap. Tier C gets research and a warm path only. Taps become Apollo enrollments, Nooks calls, TheSwarm intro requests, Centralize maps, and Salesforce tasks.

Rules for the data

The constraints the pipeline enforces, not the ones it hopes for.

  • Receipted or not stored. A signal without a source URL is rejected at ingest. The quote is stored verbatim; the model never paraphrases it into outreach.
  • Dedupe on account_id and nothing else. Ticker and domain are joins of convenience. If two rows share an id, that is a bug to fix upstream, not a merge for a rep to notice.
  • Contact data stays in the data layer. Email and mobile live in Snowflake and Apollo. A prompt gets a first name, a title, and the brief. It never sees a phone number.
  • Suppression runs twice. Once at search time through MoltSets and Clay exclude lists, so suppressed people never come back, and again immediately before any send.
  • Briefs expire. Older than 14 days, or older than the newest signal on the account, and the brief is rebuilt before a rep sees it.
  • Every touch carries its draft id and QA grade. A reply can always be traced to the exact words that earned it, which is what makes the monthly grade check possible.
  • Geography gates autonomy. Every contact carries a country. Tier A applies only in permitted geographies, EU contacts are always human-approved, and mobiles are scrubbed against do-not-call lists before any call list is built.
  • Retention follows the company's CRM policy. Vendor DPAs cover SteadyBase, MoltSets, TheSwarm, and Centralize; transcripts from Nooks keep the same retention as Gong or the call recorder already in place.

How we'd build it

One repo, six parts, three weeks to a live pod. Each part starts as a one-page spec (the interface, the tables it reads and writes, the tests that prove it), gets generated with Codex in a fresh session, and is reviewed before it merges. The models are OpenAI's; prompts and evals live in the repo, so a model change is a pull request, not a rewrite.

Zapier fits a ten-rep pilot's glue and nothing past it: per-task pricing at 100,000 touches a month costs more than the model, and it cannot host the Slack endpoint. n8n, self-hosted in the company's cloud or on n8n's cloud plan, is the flow tool that scales; it still needs someone watching it, which is why the batch work sits in Snowflake wherever it can.

Week by week, and what done looks like

Every Friday ends with a demo the pilot pod's manager can react to.

Week 1, foundationdata, plays, skeleton

The book is in Snowflake with one id per account and a match rate we can argue with. Five plays fire on real accounts. Every account has a score with three reasons. The Slack bot posts a plain card.

Friday demo: the board for one pod, ranked, with reasons. No drafts yet.

Week 2, agentsbriefs, drafts, playbook

Briefs cached for the pod's accounts. Drafts with QA grades. Verified email for most P1 contacts, and a warm path where one exists. The morning playbook runs for 10 to 20 reps. Tier A stays off.

Friday demo: a rep's morning, live, on real accounts.

Week 3, the loopexecution, replies, outcomes

Taps enroll in Apollo and land on Nooks lists. Replies are classified and drafted. Centralize is on live deals for the AE handoff. Outcomes are written. The manager view is live. Tier A turns on for SMB with caps.

Friday demo: signal to meeting, end to end, with the log beside it.

Afterpods of fifty a week

Tier boundaries tuned from the first month's replies. First weight refit at day 30. Each new pod gets the same three Friday demos, compressed into one.

Risks, and what stops them

The ones worth naming before someone else does.

AI writing to CFOs embarrasses the brand
Receipted claims only, a QA gate before any send, autonomy by tier, send caps, and a kill switch per tier and per play.
Deliverability at 500-rep volume
Caps enforced in Apollo, domain warmup, grade A and B emails only, bounces feed suppression the same hour.
A vendor is in beta, issues keys by hand, or is a very small company
MoltSets and SteadyBase both are. Apollo is the fallback on every contact row. For SteadyBase specifically: the company owns the account_id map in its own Snowflake, the resolver ladder is documented and reproducible, receipts are stored as URLs so signals outlive the vendor, and nothing downstream depends on the MCP being up.
Cold outreach rules differ by country
Contacts carry a country, and it gates autonomy. Tier A auto-send only in permitted geographies, US and Canada to start. EU contacts are always human-approved under a documented legitimate-interest basis. Business email only, never a personal-email endpoint. Mobiles are scrubbed against do-not-call lists before any Nooks list, and there is no SMS.
Reps don't use it
One tap in Slack, nothing to learn, and the manager view shows who actions signals within 24 hours.
Duplicates and wrong accounts at 1,000 reps
The resolver with a review queue, never a merge on name or domain, and territories keyed to account_id.
Prompts drift, quality slides
A golden set of briefs and emails runs in CI, prompt changes are pull requests, and QA grades are checked against reply rates monthly.
Privacy
Contact data stays out of prompts, no personal emails, DPAs with every vendor, retention matched to CRM policy.

Decisions to make together

The design changes depending on the answers.

  1. How much of the book is public. SteadyBase is shaped around SEC filers. Whatever share is private or mid-market is Clay's job, and that sets Clay's budget.
  2. Where the SteadyBase pilot stops today. If signals already land in Snowflake and Slack, the gap is wiring them into what reps do each morning, and that is most of the work.
  3. Tier boundaries. Which segments get auto-send, and what the caps are. This is a sales leadership call, not an ops one.
  4. One relationship layer or two. TheSwarm finds warm paths in bulk before contact; Centralize maps the committee once a deal is live. If budget forces one, start with TheSwarm in Clay and add Centralize for the AE handoff.
  5. Which OpenAI models, and where prompts live. GPT-5.5 for drafting and GPT-5 nano for classification to start, prompts versioned in the repo with evals, so a model change is a pull request.
